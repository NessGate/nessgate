Proposed NessGate Charter v2

Draft of 15 September 2026 · PROPOSED — NOT YET EFFECTIVE · published for public review.

Status: this is a proposal, not the rules. This page does not govern NessGate today. The active, binding charter remains Charter v1, and it stays in force unchanged until — and unless — a Charter v2 is formally adopted and published as the active charter. We publish this draft early, in the open, because we would rather show our thinking than hold it back. Nothing here describes how the live service behaves right now. Production behavior will never quietly contradict the active charter: before persistent storage, publisher registration, or resolve-time reading of a publisher's own pages ever becomes production behavior, a Charter v2 carrying these promises must first become the active published charter.

Why a v2 is proposed

NessGate today reads only what a domain publishes at its exact host, live, storing nothing. That is trustworthy, but on measurement it returns nothing for most domains — useful resources often live on a subdomain, a related property, or a public registry the strict resolver never reaches. A resolver that is empty most of the time will be routed around. The proposal below lets NessGate find and normalize more of what genuinely exists, without loosening what "verified" or "official" means: every result still says exactly why it exists, and weak evidence is never presented as authoritative. The full technical design is public in docs/v2-architecture.md.

Retained from Charter v1 (unchanged)

These promises carry over verbatim and are not up for revision:

  • Free forever to participate — no fee to use the resolver, to be read, or to build on it.
  • Never sell ranking, placement, priority, or badges — there is nothing to buy that changes how a domain is treated.
  • Neutral to everyone — every domain is read the same way and returned the same way.
  • The resource always lives on the domain — NessGate reads and links back; it never rehosts, owns, or becomes the resource.
  • Open and independently implementable — public specification, Apache-2.0 reference implementation, buildable by anyone without permission.
  • A stable, additive specification — a published response shape keeps working across a major version; breaking changes only ever arrive as a new major version.

What would change (v1 → v2), and why

1. From "stores nothing" to two clearly separated, inspectable kinds of state

Charter v1 says: "Nothing is crawled, indexed, or persisted. Each answer is computed fresh and dies with its short edge cache."

Charter v2 would promise: NessGate keeps two clearly separated kinds of state, both inspectable — records a publisher declared or registered, kept until the publisher revokes them and re-verified on a schedule; and a short-lived discovery cache re-derived from public evidence and never served stale. NessGate builds no content corpus — no stored page text, no search index — only pointers, each carrying its source, evidence class, verification state, timestamps, and expiry.

2. From "no domain data" to publisher-owned and independently-attributed records

Charter v1 says: "NessGate keeps no user data and no domain data."

Charter v2 would promise: No accounts and no user data, ever. A domain record exists only because the domain itself declared or registered it, or because an independent public source (a certificate log, another publisher, a named registry) attested it with recorded provenance. Publishers control what they own and may revoke it at any time; independent evidence is separately attributed and expires on its own schedule rather than being erased on demand.

3. A bounded, publisher-scoped resolve-time read

New clause: In its broader tiers NessGate may read a publisher's own public pages at resolve time — bounded, robots-respecting, with provenance on every hop — solely to follow that publisher's own links. It does not crawl arbitrary third-party sites or commentary about a publisher. Separately, a small set of explicitly defined public adapters (certificate-transparency logs, named registries) may read third-party sources, always with full provenance and always as discovered (Level 2) until independently confirmed.

4. Reaffirmed under the new tiers: no scores, no ranking

Charter v1 says: "It does not rank, score, rate…"

Charter v2 would promise: No scores, ever — no numeric confidence, no ranking. Results are grouped by evidence class and deterministically ordered within each group; grouping is not ranking and buys no one placement.

New affirmative promises

Charter v2 would add these explicit commitments:

  1. Discovery never becomes authority. A resource NessGate merely found is marked discovered and stays there; it becomes authoritative only when a publisher declaration, a verified registration, or a domain-authenticated registry independently establishes it. Storing a discovered record never upgrades it.
  2. Two questions, never merged. NessGate reports whether a resource is real and how strongly it relates to a domain as two separate facts. Finding or verifying a resource never makes it "official."
  3. Storage independence. Any persistent store is an implementation choice, never part of the protocol. The same captured evidence, evaluated under the same open rules, yields the same classification with any store or none.
  4. Operator independence. nessgate.com is one reference deployment. The open implementation runs for anyone on their own database or with no database, and reaches the same classifications from the same captured evidence and the same open rules.
  5. No privileged submitter — including our own commercial layer. Anyone may submit candidate resources; every submission, from any party including NessReady, is evaluated under the identical public rules and remains discovered until it independently earns authority. There is no private path to promotion, and none for sale.
  6. Source-preserving federation. When a result comes from another registry, NessGate names that registry and its verification method. Registries are never flattened into a generic "trusted" stamp.
  7. No proprietary publishing format. NessGate introduces no publishing file for domains to adopt. It reads the standards that already exist; a domain's own standard files are its declaration.

Before v2 could become active

This draft becomes binding only when it is formally adopted and published here as the active charter, replacing v1. Until then: v1 governs; any pre-release or experimental build that exercises a v2-only capability will be labelled experimental and will name exactly which proposed v2 capabilities it uses, and will never be presented as governed by v1. The single hard rule is that production behavior must never quietly contradict the active charter — so persistent storage, registration, and resolve-time page reading do not go live in production before Charter v2 is the active charter.
